# Told Straight — Evidence Brief: The Coping Side of ADHD + Schema Work
### Prepared 2026-07-29 | Every claim graded; every DOI verified in-session or explicitly flagged

**Scope note (candor first):** This brief grades the *coping techniques* the episode teaches. Two honesty flags apply throughout: (a) almost none of this evidence base was generated *in ADHD-plus-schema populations specifically* — most is borrowed from depression/anxiety/personality-disorder or general-population research, and every extrapolation is flagged; (b) each DOI below marked "verified" was checked against the live literature during the research session. Where an exact DOI could not be confirmed, it says so explicitly rather than printing a guess.

**Grading key:** STRONG (multiple RCTs/meta-analyses) · MODERATE (some controlled studies) · WEAK (uncontrolled/cross-sectional) · CLINICAL-CONCEPT (used clinically, little peer-reviewed support)

## Section 1 — Metacognition / the "think about your thinking" skill

**Flavell's definition (the concept's origin).** The term was coined by developmental psychologist John Flavell: metacognition is "cognition about cognitive phenomena" — knowledge and monitoring of one's own thinking.
- Flavell, J. H. (1979). *American Psychologist*, 34(10), 906–911. DOI: 10.1037/0003-066X.34.10.906 — **verified.**
- **Grade: CLINICAL-CONCEPT / foundational theory.** This is a definition, not an intervention. It underwrites the *skill* the episode teaches (noticing a thought without acting on it) but is not itself "evidence that the skill works."

**Adrian Wells' Metacognitive Therapy (MCT) and the Cognitive Attentional Syndrome (CAS).** Wells' model: distress is maintained not by the *content* of thoughts but by a style of responding to them — the CAS: perseverative worry/rumination, threat-monitoring, unhelpful coping — driven by metacognitive beliefs. MCT targets that process directly (detached mindfulness, attention training).
- Efficacy evidence: **Normann & Morina (2018), *Frontiers in Psychology*, 9:2211. DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02211 — verified.** Systematic review/meta-analysis. Large effects for MCT vs. waitlist (Hedges' g ≈ 2.06) and a moderate advantage over other active treatments including standard CBT (g ≈ 0.37–0.69).
- **Grade: MODERATE-to-STRONG for anxiety and depression** — multiple RCTs and a positive meta-analysis, with real caveats to voice: small trial samples, several trials run by MCT-affiliated groups, heterogeneity. The g≈2.06 vs. waitlist is inflated by waitlist being a weak comparator; the honest headline is the ~0.4–0.7 edge over active treatments.
- **CRUCIAL CAVEAT (say on air): MCT has ZERO randomized trials in ADHD.** Its evidence base is anxiety and depression. Applying Wells' MCT to ADHD is a reasonable-sounding extrapolation, not an evidenced treatment.

**Mary Solanto's "metacognitive therapy" for adult ADHD — and why the name misleads.**
- Solanto, M. V., et al. (2010). *American Journal of Psychiatry*, 167(8), 958–968. DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.2009.09081123 — **verified.** RCT, N=88 adults with ADHD, 12-week manualized group intervention vs. time-and-attention-matched supportive psychotherapy. Result: significantly greater reductions in inattentive symptoms (CAARS, Brown ADD Scales); ~47% fell below the inattentive clinical cutoff post-treatment. First non-medication treatment for adult ADHD shown to beat a matched control.
- **Clarify plainly:** despite the label, Solanto's program is **CBT-based executive-skills training** — time management, organization, planning — *not* Wells' MCT and *not* about "thinking about your thinking" in the Flavell/Wells sense. Flagged in print at the time: a commentary titled "Meta-Cognitive Therapy Without Metacognition: A Case of ADHD" (*Am J Psychiatry* 2010; DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2010.10101467 — title verified; it is a letter/commentary). Two different things wear the same name.
- **Grade: MODERATE** for Solanto's program (one well-designed matched-control RCT; replication and long-term/functional outcomes thinner).

## Section 2 — Schema therapy outcomes, and the "NAME it / decenter" move

**Does schema therapy work (in general)?**
- Landmark RCT: **Bamelis, L. L., et al. (2014). *American Journal of Psychiatry*, 171(3), 305–322.** N=323, multicenter; schema therapy vs. treatment-as-usual vs. clarification-oriented psychotherapy for cluster-C, paranoid, histrionic, narcissistic personality disorders. Schema therapy: higher recovery rates, greater functional gains, lower dropout. *(Title, journal, volume, N verified. DOI commonly cited as 10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.12040518 — confirm this exact string against the AJP page before publishing; the article itself is confirmed real.)*
- Meta-analysis: **2023 systematic review/meta-analysis, *Nordic Journal of Psychiatry*, 77(7). DOI: 10.1080/08039488.2023.2228304 — verified.** 8 RCTs (587 participants) + 7 single-group trials; schema therapy showed a **moderate effect, g ≈ 0.36** vs. control for personality-disorder symptoms.
- **Grade: MODERATE.** Real RCT support for personality disorders, but the meta-analytic effect is moderate (~0.36), trials are few, and some were run by the approach's developers.
- **Say clearly: little-to-no RCT evidence for schema therapy *in ADHD specifically.*** The three-move protocol is a clinically sensible adaptation, not an ADHD-validated treatment.

**Does the "NAME it / decenter" step have support?** It maps onto two evidenced mechanisms:
- **Cognitive defusion (ACT)** — distance from a thought ("I'm having the thought that I'm unlovable" vs. "I'm unlovable") — exactly "this is my schema, not a fact." **Levin, M. E., et al. (2012). *Behavior Therapy*, 43(4), 741–756. DOI: 10.1016/j.beth.2012.05.003 — verified.** Meta-analysis of 66 laboratory component studies; defusion (with acceptance, present-moment, values) showed significant positive effects vs. inactive comparisons. **Grade: MODERATE** — reliable lab effects, but short-term, non-clinical samples, not ADHD.
- **Cognitive restructuring within CBT for adult ADHD** — the "catch and challenge the thought" family is a core ingredient of CBT packages with ADHD RCT support. **Grade for CBT-for-adult-ADHD as a whole: STRONG** (multiple RCTs + meta-analysis); for *the decentering component in isolation*: MODERATE (rarely dismantled out).
  - **Knouse, L. E., Teller, J., & Brooks, M. A. (2017). *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*, 85(7), 737–750. DOI: 10.1037/ccp0000216 — verified.** Meta-analysis, ~32 studies, up to 896 participants; moderate effects vs. control on global functioning, moderate-to-large at follow-up. (A published correction exists — Tables 1–2/Figs 2–3 miscoding — authors state conclusions unchanged.)

## Section 3 — Behavioral experiments / "BREAK the pattern"

**Behavioral experiments (do-the-thing-anyway, let reality vote).** Recognized core CBT technique; arguably the most potent route to belief change.
- Systematic review: **McMillan, D., & Lee, R. (2010). *Clinical Psychology Review*, 30(5), 467–478** — behavioral experiments vs. exposure alone in anxiety disorders. Cautiously favorable to behavioral experiments; methodological limits precluded firm conclusions. *(Title/journal verified; confirm exact page/DOI before publishing.)*
- Belief-change experiments: behavioral experiments change target beliefs *earlier* and generalize more than thought records alone (McManus, Van Doorn & Yiend, 2012, *J. Behavior Therapy & Experimental Psychiatry*) — supports the "reality vote" claim.
- **Grade: MODERATE.** Widely used, mechanistically well-supported, rarely isolated in large RCTs; most evidence is in anxiety, not ADHD.

**Behavioral activation (scheduling and doing valued action regardless of mood/motivation).** Directly relevant to "I know what to do, I just can't start."
- **Ekers, D., et al. (2014). *PLoS ONE*, 9(6), e100100. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0100100 — verified.** Meta-analysis, 26 RCTs, ~1,524 participants; BA superior to controls (SMD ≈ −0.74) and to medication (SMD ≈ −0.42) for depression.
- **Grade: STRONG for depression.** Be candid: this is a **depression** evidence base; no comparable BA meta-analysis exists *for ADHD*. Using it as an anti-avoidance scaffold in ADHD is well-reasoned extrapolation, not a validated ADHD treatment.

## Section 4 — Self-compassion and ADHD (shame / self-criticism)

- **Beaton, D. M., Sirois, F., & Milne, E. (2020). "Self-compassion and perceived criticism in adults with ADHD." *Mindfulness.*** Cross-sectional: adults with ADHD are significantly *less* self-compassionate than non-ADHD adults, partly explained by higher perceived criticism from others. *(Title/authors verified; confirm exact volume/DOI before publishing.)*
- **Beaton, D. M., Sirois, F., & Milne, E. (2022). *Journal of Clinical Psychology*, 78(12). DOI: 10.1002/jclp.23354 — verified.** Higher self-compassion predicts better mental health and buffers negative psychosocial outcomes in adults with ADHD; positions self-compassion as a treatment target.
- **An RCT is in progress** (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04978792, "Does cultivating self-compassion improve resilience to criticism… in adults with ADHD") — verified as registered; the interventional evidence is not yet in.
- **Grade: WEAK (for ADHD specifically).** The existing ADHD evidence is **cross-sectional/correlational** — self-compassion is *associated with* better outcomes and is *lower* in ADHD; no trial yet shows an intervention *causes* improvement in ADHD. (The general self-compassion-intervention literature outside ADHD is stronger — different population.) On-air framing: "strong theoretical fit and good correlational signal; the causal trial is literally still running."

## Section 5 — Practical coping moves per schema

### 5a. Insufficient Self-Control / Self-Discipline

- **Implementation intentions ("if X, then I will Y").** **Gollwitzer, P. M., & Sheeran, P. (2006). *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 38, 69–119. DOI: 10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1 — verified.** Meta-analysis of 94 independent tests; medium-to-large effect on goal attainment, **d ≈ 0.65.** **Grade: STRONG** — with the caveat that it's general goal-pursuit research, not ADHD trials; ADHD-specific effects are less established and may be attenuated by forgetting/initiation deficits.
- **External scaffolding / environmental design (offloading executive function to the environment).** The operating principle behind CBT-for-adult-ADHD packages with RCT support (Solanto 2010; Safren 2010; Knouse 2017). **Grade: MODERATE-to-STRONG as embedded in those packages;** WEAK as an isolated, separately-tested tactic.
  - **Safren, S. A., et al. (2010). *JAMA*, 304(8), 875–880. DOI: 10.1001/jama.2010.1192 — verified.** RCT, N=86 medication-treated adults with persistent ADHD symptoms; CBT (skills/scaffolding) beat relaxation-with-education; gains maintained to 9 months.
- **Body-doubling (working alongside another person).** **Grade: CLINICAL-CONCEPT / WEAK.** Popular in ADHD communities and commercial platforms (Focusmate etc.), plausible mechanisms (social facilitation, implicit accountability), but **no rigorous controlled trials** — anecdote and very small/emerging studies only (e.g., a 12-participant VR pilot, arXiv 2509.12153). **Flag on air as promising-but-unproven.**
- **Task-interest engineering (gamifying, novelty, urgency to exploit interest-driven attention).** **Grade: WEAK / theory-linked.** Consistent with dopamine/motivation accounts of ADHD; direct intervention evidence thin. Present as reasonable hypothesis, not established.

### 5b. Emotional Deprivation

- Coping moves: identifying and *voicing* needs; building reciprocal support. **Grade: CLINICAL-CONCEPT.** This is **clinical consensus, not RCT-validated for this schema.** Adjacent indirect support: social support robustly predicts better mental-health outcomes (broad epidemiological literature); expressing needs overlaps with assertiveness (5c). No trial shows "ask for your needs" resolves the Emotional Deprivation schema. Say so.

### 5c. Self-Sacrifice

- Coping move: assertiveness / boundary-setting. **Grade: MODERATE (general), LIMITED direct evidence for the Self-Sacrifice schema.**
  - **Speed, B. C., Goldstein, B. L., & Goldfried, M. R. (2018). "Assertiveness Training: A Forgotten Evidence-Based Treatment." *Clinical Psychology: Science and Practice.*** Decades of evidence: assertiveness training improves social skills and self-esteem, reduces social anxiety/depression. *(Title/authors verified; confirm exact volume/DOI before publishing.)*
  - Recent RCT: transdiagnostic CBT for assertiveness (Kabuki et al., 2023, *J. Behavior Therapy & Experimental Psychiatry* / PMC10235435) — verified as a real RCT.
  - **Honest flag:** measured outcomes are social anxiety, self-esteem, assertive behavior — **not "reduced self-sacrificing schema behavior."** The link from assertiveness training to breaking a Self-Sacrifice schema is inferential.

## Fact-checker's honesty ledger

- **Not verified to exact DOI (confirm before print):** Bamelis 2014 DOI string; McMillan & Lee 2010 page/DOI; Beaton 2020 (*Mindfulness*) volume/DOI; Speed et al. 2018 volume/DOI. The *articles* are real and title-verified; only the identifier strings need a final check.
- **Biggest "popular-but-unproven" flags for the episode:** (1) Wells' MCT has **no ADHD trials** — its name-sharing with Solanto's program is a genuine source of public confusion; (2) **body-doubling** is anecdote-grade; (3) **self-compassion for ADHD** is correlational only (causal RCT still running); (4) coping moves for **Emotional Deprivation** and **Self-Sacrifice** are **clinical consensus, not schema-specific RCT evidence**; (5) the strongest numbers (behavioral activation, implementation intentions) come from **depression/general-population** research, extrapolated to ADHD.
- **Strongest ground to stand on:** CBT/skills-training for adult ADHD (Knouse meta + Safren + Solanto RCTs) and implementation intentions (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, d≈0.65). Those claims survive fact-checking cleanly.
- No citation in this brief is invented; every DOI marked "verified" was confirmed against the source during the research session, and every unconfirmed identifier is flagged rather than presented as certain.
