## 1. Premise and continuity

Ep02 ended with homework: take the Young Schema Questionnaire. Ep03 opens with the
homework **done** — a real, filled-out YSQ-L3 (232 items, dated 29 JUL 26, margin
notes and all) — and brings in Dr. Anna Sinclair to review the results with Jared.

Carryover spine (Des's Swiss army knife from Ep02, explicitly recalled): the
plain-English metacognitive skill — *thinking about your thinking; you don't have to
grab every thought the firehose throws* — is the same blade that powers Ep02's
three-move schema protocol: **NAME it → TRACE it → BREAK the pattern on purpose.**
Ep03 runs that protocol live, on real results.

The candor frame is double-layered this episode and both layers are content:
1. Jared is sharing real, sensitive psychometric results with a broad audience —
   deliberate, on-brand, said out loud.
2. Anna is not real — and *she* says so (heightened-disclosure rule, card 005) —
   while Jared is seeing his actual doctor about the same results. The episode can
   land the comparison: what the synthetic clinician said vs. what the real one did.

## 3. Segment arc (~6 segments, Ep02-like length)

**S1 — Cold open + personnel file (≈1.5 min).** Jared: the homework came back.
Anna's intro = card 005 verbatim device: role, "License number: NOT-A-REAL-1",
**her own-voice disclosure** (composite, orientation-not-diagnosis, "and Jared is
seeing his actual doctor — today, in fact"). NOT REAL stamp on her art.

**S2 — How to read a YSQ without scaring yourself (≈2 min).** Anna explains the
instrument: 18 schemas, 1–6 scale, "answer from what you feel." The evidence-grade
caveat up front: the adult schema×ADHD literature is **two small studies that
disagree** (Philipsen 2017; Kiraz 2021) — the show's motto applied to its own
premise. Rule of thumb: means are noise, 5s and 6s are signal.

**S3 — The headline: Emotional Deprivation, 4.56 (≈3 min).** Seven items at 5.
Anna's beats: (a) best-supported of his three — top-four elevated schema in
Philipsen; (b) the substrate is STRONG science — peer rejection ~50% vs ~14%
(Hoza 2005, MTA), emotion dysregulation as core feature (Shaw 2014) — with the
schema pathway labeled inference, not proof; (c) the profile twist: classic
presentations pair this with Defectiveness/Shame — Jared's Defectiveness is 1.13,
Failure 1.00. His margin notes carry it: "I have a few people like that, but nobody
close enough physically." Supply problem, not self problem. NAME → TRACE (the
undiagnosed decades) → BREAK (the ask-for-what-you-need experiment, honestly graded
clinical-consensus).

**S4 — The fingerprint: Insufficient Self-Control, 3.13 (≈3 min).** The shape, not
the score: the form's only 6 — item 189, *"I can't force myself to do things I
don't enjoy, even when I know it's for my own good"* — plus 5s on boring-task
discipline and boredom, while anger and concentration items floor at 1. Anna:
schema-level evidence is contested (top-4 in Kiraz, possibly non-significant in
Philipsen — say the studies disagree), but the mechanism under it is the strongest
science in the episode: delay discounting meta-analyses (Marx 2021, N=3,763;
Jackson & MacKillop 2016), Barkley's inhibition model, Sonuga-Barke delay aversion,
Sergeant state regulation (performance collapses specifically when stimulation
does). Then the graded verdicts, told straight: **"interest-based nervous system"**
(Dodson) — not in DSM-5-TR, no validated measure, but rhymes with real science;
**RSD** — not in DSM, a 4-patient case series, and the famous 98–99% stat has no
published dataset. The reframe: item 189 isn't a character flaw scored 6, it's
state-dependent regulation scored honestly.

**S5 — The compensation: Self-Sacrifice, 3.12 (≈2.5 min).** 5s on guilt, giving
more than getting, resident caretaker, always finding time for others. Anna's
candor beat: **weakest-evidenced of the three** — never a standout in either study;
masking/over-functioning literature is qualitative and mostly in women; the
caretaking-as-compensation story is clinical concept, not established science. And
the interlock that writes itself: giving at 5s, receiving at 5s-of-absence — the
Self-Sacrifice pattern hides the Emotional Deprivation deficit. Margin notes as
script: "just don't know any other way" / "getting better, had habit."

**S6 — The coping plan + the real doctor (≈3 min).** The knife carries over: one
blade, three schemas, each move graded out loud (brief 2):
- **Insufficient Self-Control:** implementation intentions — STRONG, d≈0.65
  (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, 94 tests); external scaffolding as embedded in
  CBT-for-ADHD packages — MODERATE-to-STRONG (Safren 2010 JAMA; Solanto 2010;
  Knouse 2017 meta); body-doubling — CLINICAL-CONCEPT, anecdote-grade, say so;
  task-interest engineering — plausible, thin.
- **Emotional Deprivation:** identify and voice needs, build reciprocity —
  clinical consensus, not RCT-proven for this schema; adjacent support evidence
  real.
- **Self-Sacrifice:** assertiveness training — MODERATE in general (Speed 2018),
  inferential for this schema specifically.
- **Self-compassion for ADHD shame:** correlational only; the causal RCT is
  literally still recruiting (NCT04978792) — "strong theoretical fit, trial still
  running" is the on-air line.
Close: Jared saw his real doctor the same day he scored this. The comparison —
where the composite clinician and the real one agreed and differed — is the
episode's final candor beat and the Ep04 hook.

**Homework:** one implementation intention (if-then, written), one voiced need,
one declined obligation. Three experiments, one week, reality votes.
