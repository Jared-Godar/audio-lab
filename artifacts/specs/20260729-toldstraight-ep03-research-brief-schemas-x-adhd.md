# Evidence Brief — Early Maladaptive Schemas & Adult ADHD
### For "Told Straight" | Prepared 2026-07-29 | Every empirical claim carries a verified DOI/URL and an evidence grade

**Grading key:** STRONG (multiple RCTs/meta-analyses) · MODERATE (some controlled studies) · WEAK (uncontrolled/cross-sectional only) · CLINICAL-CONCEPT (widely used by clinicians, little/no peer-reviewed support)

**A blunt framing note for the host:** The schema-to-ADHD literature is thin — two small cross-sectional case-control studies carry almost the entire "which schema" question, and they *disagree* on the ranking of the three schemas you care about. Everything downstream (self-regulation science, emotion dysregulation, peer rejection) is far better evidenced than the schema mapping itself. Where the schema link is inferred rather than measured, this brief says so explicitly.

---

## Section 1 — Which schemas are empirically elevated in adult ADHD?

**Overall grade for this question: WEAK-to-MODERATE.** Two small case-control studies, both cross-sectional, no RCTs, no longitudinal or mechanistic work, and they conflict on rankings. Neither abstract reports effect sizes (Cohen's *d* / η²) — only group significance and rank order. Full-text results tables were not obtainable this session; treat all "most elevated" claims as rank-order, not magnitude.

### Study 1 — Philipsen, Lam et al. 2017
Philipsen A, Lam AP, Breit S, Lücke C, Müller HH, Matthies S (2017). "Early maladaptive schemas in adult patients with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder." *Attention Deficit and Hyperactivity Disorders* 9(2):101–111. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12402-016-0211-8 (PMID 28012033). **[MODERATE for existence of elevation; WEAK for specificity]**

- Design: case-control, **78 adult ADHD patients vs 80 controls**, Young Schema Questionnaire (YSQ).
- Headline result (verbatim from abstract): ADHD patients scored significantly higher on **almost all** schemas; the **four most pronounced were Failure, Defectiveness/Shame, Subjugation, and Emotional Deprivation.**
- **Emotional Deprivation: significantly elevated — and among the top four. This is the strongest single data point for the Emotional Deprivation thesis.**
- **Insufficient Self-Control and Self-Sacrifice: NOT among the pronounced schemas here** — and there is a source conflict on whether they reached significance at all (see caveat below).

⚠️ **Fact-check flag — an unresolved discrepancy in the secondary literature.** The *primary abstract* (Springer/PubMed) states the only schema that did **not** differ between groups was **Vulnerability to Harm or Illness** — implying Insufficient Self-Control and Self-Sacrifice *were* significantly elevated (just not top-ranked). But a secondary review paraphrase (from the same research group's follow-up, Lücke, Lam, Müller & Philipsen, *J Neurol Neuromed* 2017;2(7):6–10) lists **five** non-significant schemas: Vulnerability to Harm, Enmeshment/Undeveloped Self, **Self-Sacrifice, Insufficient Self-Control,** and Approval-Seeking. The full results table was not accessible to adjudicate which correction threshold produces which list. **On air, say: "significantly elevated overall, but not among the standout schemas, and the sources disagree on whether those two even cleared significance."** Do not assert a clean effect size for Insufficient Self-Control or Self-Sacrifice from this study — it isn't in the verifiable record.

### Study 2 — Kiraz & Sertçelik 2021
Kiraz S, Sertçelik S (2021). "Adult attention deficit hyperactivity disorder and early maladaptive schemas." *Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy* 28(5):1055–1064. https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.2569 (PMID 33586830). **[MODERATE for existence of elevation; WEAK for specificity]**

- Design: case-control, **55 adult ADHD vs 52 controls**, ages 18–32, YSQ-S3, diagnosis confirmed by SCID + DIVA.
- Headline result (verbatim): **all** maladaptive schemas were significantly higher in the ADHD group; the **four most prominent were Failure, Emotional Inhibition, Insufficient Self-Control, and Social Isolation.**
- **Insufficient Self-Control: here it IS top-four** — the opposite of its standing in Philipsen. This is the crux of the between-study disagreement.
- **Emotional Deprivation and Self-Sacrifice: elevated (everything was) but not standout.** Least prominent were Approval-Seeking, Entitlement/Grandiosity, and Punitiveness.
- No effect sizes reported in the abstract.

### Where the three target schemas actually rank — the honest synthesis

| Schema | Philipsen 2017 (n=78) | Kiraz 2021 (n=55) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emotional Deprivation** | **Top-4 (standout)** | Elevated, not standout | **Most consistently supported of the three.** MODERATE |
| **Insufficient Self-Control** | Not standout; possibly non-sig | **Top-4 (standout)** | **Genuinely disputed between studies.** WEAK — the two best studies contradict each other |
| **Self-Sacrifice** | Not standout; possibly non-sig | Elevated, not standout | **Weakest of the three.** WEAK — never a standout in either study |

**Newer sources checked (context only, not adult-ADHD case-control):** a 2024 *Frontiers in Psychiatry* study on EMS in **adolescents** with ADHD (https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1455897), a 2024 *Frontiers in Psychology* conceptual review on EMS in autistic/ADHD individuals (https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1436053), and a 2025 study on schema mechanisms in comorbid ADHD+BPD (https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.70206, PMID 41408911). None supersedes Philipsen/Kiraz as the primary adult case-control evidence; the adolescent and comorbid work is not a clean substitute. **The adult EMS-ADHD base is still essentially two small studies. That is the candor-worthy headline.**

## Section 2 — Insufficient Self-Control schema ↔ ADHD executive dysfunction

**This is where the real science lives.** The schema literature is thin, but the underlying self-regulation science is STRONG. The move for the show: the "Insufficient Self-Control schema" is a clinical label sitting on top of a large, well-evidenced executive-function/motivation literature.

### Barkley's behavioral-inhibition / self-regulation model — STRONG as theory, foundational
Barkley RA (1997). "Behavioral inhibition, sustained attention, and executive functions: Constructing a unifying theory of ADHD." *Psychological Bulletin* 121(1):65–94. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.121.1.65 **[STRONG as an influential, heavily replicated framework — 7,000+ citations]**

- Core claim: ADHD is fundamentally a deficit in **behavioral inhibition**, which cascades into four executive functions: working memory, **self-regulation of affect/motivation/arousal**, internalization of speech, and reconstitution. The model itself places *emotional* self-regulation inside the core deficit — a useful bridge to Section 3.
- Caveat for candor: it's a unifying *theory*; meta-analyses show EF deficits are real but **not universal** in ADHD (roughly 30–50% of individuals show clinically significant EF impairment on any given task), which is why single-pathway models gave way to dual-pathway models below.

### Delay aversion & delay discounting (Sonuga-Barke and others) — MODERATE-to-STRONG
- **Dual-pathway model:** Sonuga-Barke EJS (2002/2003). "The dual pathway model of AD/HD: an elaboration of neuro-developmental characteristics." *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 27(7):593–604. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneubiorev.2003.08.005 **[MODERATE-STRONG]** — two dissociable routes: cognitive (inhibition/EF) *and* motivational (**delay aversion** — an emotionally driven preference to escape/avoid delay). Explains why not everyone with ADHD shows EF deficits.
- **Delay-discounting meta-analysis:** Marx I, Hacker T, Yu X, Cortese S, Sonuga-Barke E (2021). "ADHD and the Choice of Small Immediate Over Larger Delayed Rewards: A Comparative Meta-Analysis…" *Journal of Attention Disorders* 25(2):171–187. https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054718772138 **[STRONG]** — 37 group comparisons, **3,763 participants**, **small-to-medium effect sizes** for ADHD choosing small-immediate over large-delayed rewards; offering *real* (vs hypothetical) rewards nearly doubled the odds ratio.
- **Second confirmatory meta-analysis:** Jackson JNS & MacKillop J (2016). "Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder and Monetary Delay Discounting: A Meta-Analysis of Case-Control Studies." *Biological Psychiatry: CNNI* 1(4):316–325. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2016.01.007 **[STRONG]** — independently confirms steeper discounting in ADHD.

### Task/interest-dependence — performance depends on the task, not just the person — MODERATE
Sergeant JA (2005). "Modeling attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: a critical appraisal of the cognitive-energetic model." *Biological Psychiatry* 57(11):1248–1255. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2004.09.010 **[MODERATE]** — the **state-regulation / cognitive-energetic** account: ADHD performance deficits are worst in **slow, boring, under-stimulating** conditions and can normalize under fast event rates / stimulating conditions. This is the peer-reviewed backbone under the popular "it's not that they can't focus, it's that focus is state-dependent" talking point. (A 2026 *Clinical Child & Family Psychology Review* narrative+meta review on the "boredom-ADHD nexus," https://doi.org/10.1007/s10567-026-00563-9, extends this — verify before citing; not read in full this session.)

### The two popular clinician concepts — graded honestly

**(a) "Interest-Based Nervous System" (William Dodson) — CLINICAL-CONCEPT (essentially no primary peer-reviewed evidence)**
- Origin: psychiatrist **William W. Dodson, MD**, popularized largely through *ADDitude* magazine (e.g., https://www.additudemag.com/adhd-brain-chemistry-video/), not through a peer-reviewed primary paper. The idea — ADHD attention is governed by interest/novelty/challenge/urgency rather than importance/priority — is a **reframing/heuristic**, and it *rhymes* with the peer-reviewed state-regulation and delay-aversion science above.
- **DSM-5-TR: NO.** It is not a diagnostic construct.
- Evidence: no validated measure, no controlled studies of "interest-based nervous system" as a defined construct. **The defensible on-air statement: "The clinical observation maps onto real, well-evidenced science (state regulation, delay aversion), but 'interest-based nervous system' itself is a popularizer's framing, not a studied, measured construct."**

**(b) "Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)" — CLINICAL-CONCEPT / WEAK**
- Origin: coined/popularized by **William W. Dodson, MD** (~2017), again primarily via *ADDitude* and talks, describing extreme emotional pain triggered by perceived rejection. Dodson's widely-quoted claim that "98–99% of adolescents and adults with ADHD experience RSD" **has no published dataset behind it that could be verified — treat that figure as unsubstantiated.**
- **DSM-5-TR: NO.** RSD is not a DSM diagnosis, code, or specifier.
- Peer-reviewed evidence base is very small and low-tier:
  - Dodson WW, Modestino EJ, et al. (2024). "Rejection Sensitivity Dysphoria in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: A Case Series." *Acta Scientific Neurology* 7(8):23–30. https://actascientific.com/ASNE/pdf/ASNE-07-0762.pdf — **a 4-patient case series** in a low-impact/predatory-adjacent journal. **[WEAK]**
  - Bedrossian L (2021). "Understand and address complexities of rejection sensitive dysphoria in students with ADHD." *Disability Compliance for Higher Education*. https://doi.org/10.1002/dhe.31047 — practitioner commentary, not empirical. **[CLINICAL-CONCEPT]**
  - A 2024 preprint, "The lived experience of rejection sensitivity in ADHD" (medRxiv, https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.11.16.24317418) — **not yet peer-reviewed**; qualitative.
- Independent appraisals note there are only a handful of studies, all small (n ≈ 4–43), all qualitative, and **no validated RSD measure exists** — so it's unclear whether RSD is distinct from established constructs like rejection sensitivity (Downey & Feldman) or emotion dysregulation. **Grade: CLINICAL-CONCEPT bordering WEAK. The honest line: "A useful clinical description of something real patients report — but as a named syndrome it is unvalidated, unmeasured, not in the DSM, and the famous 99% statistic has no published data behind it."**
- Important contrast for the show: **emotion dysregulation in ADHD (Section 3) IS well-evidenced.** RSD is one clinician's branded, under-tested slice of that well-evidenced phenomenon. Don't let the weak label discredit the strong underlying finding.

## Section 3 — Emotional Deprivation schema ↔ ADHD

**The schema-specific link is inferential. The surrounding phenomena (emotion dysregulation, peer rejection, relationship difficulty) are well-evidenced.** State that separation plainly.

### Emotion dysregulation in ADHD — STRONG
Shaw P, Stringaris A, Nigg J, Leibenluft E (2014). "Emotion Dysregulation in Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder." *American Journal of Psychiatry* 171(3):276–293. https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2013.13070966 **[STRONG — authoritative review]**

- Emotion dysregulation is **prevalent across the lifespan** in ADHD and a **major independent contributor to impairment**, not merely a byproduct.
- Proposes a striato-amygdalo-medial-prefrontal network basis; frames dysregulation as arising partly from deficits in orienting/allocating attention to emotional stimuli.
- **Relevance to Emotional Deprivation schema:** this establishes that ADHD involves genuine difficulty processing and regulating emotion — a plausible *mechanism* by which emotional needs go unrecognized/unmet in relationships. But **Shaw does not study the Emotional Deprivation schema.** The bridge from "emotion dysregulation" to "Emotional Deprivation schema" is **inferential.**

### Peer rejection across the lifespan — STRONG (childhood)
Hoza B, Mrug S, Gerdes AC, et al. (2005). "What aspects of peer relationships are impaired in children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder?" *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology* 73(3):411–423. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-006X.73.3.411 **[STRONG]**

- From the MTA sample: **~48–52% of children with ADHD were rejected by peers vs ~14% of classmates**, and ~56% had no reciprocal friend vs ~32% of classmates.
- Longitudinal cascade evidence: Murray-Close D, Hoza B, et al. (2010), *Development and Psychopathology* — MTA peer-problem developmental cascades. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954579410000313 **[MODERATE-STRONG]**
- **Inference to schema:** early, pervasive rejection is exactly the developmental substrate schema theory posits for Emotional Deprivation/Defectiveness — but no study directly tests "childhood ADHD peer rejection → adult Emotional Deprivation schema." **Inferential.**

### Relationship / marital difficulty — MODERATE
- Adults with ADHD show elevated relationship conflict and lower satisfaction; longitudinal follow-up work (Barkley RA, Murphy KR, Fischer M, *ADHD in Adults: What the Science Says*, Guilford 2008) documents higher separation/divorce and relationship instability. **[MODERATE — book synthesis of longitudinal data, not a single RCT]**
- Eakin L, et al. (2004), *Journal of Attention Disorders* 8(1):1–10 (marital/family dysfunction in adult ADHD), https://doi.org/10.1177/108705470400800101 **[MODERATE — controlled but small]**
- Caveat: much popular "ADHD divorce rate" content (e.g., ADDitude, CHADD) cites figures like "60% marital maladjustment" that trace to clinical/survey sources, not clean epidemiology. Cite the peer-reviewed studies, not the magazine percentages.

**Section 3 bottom line for the host:** "ADHD is robustly linked to emotion dysregulation, lifelong peer rejection, and relationship strain — that's STRONG-to-MODERATE. The leap to Young's *Emotional Deprivation schema* specifically is a clinically reasonable inference, and Philipsen found that schema elevated — but no study has directly traced the developmental pathway. Label it inference, not proof."

## Section 4 — Self-Sacrifice / caretaking / "masking" / compensatory over-functioning

**Say it plainly on air: this is the weakest-evidenced of the three schema links, and much of the "masking / people-pleasing / over-functioning" narrative is clinical lore and qualitative report, not controlled evidence.**

- **Self-Sacrifice schema ↔ ADHD directly: essentially unstudied.** Neither Philipsen nor Kiraz found Self-Sacrifice to be a standout schema; in Philipsen it may not have reached significance at all (Section 1 caveat). **Grade: WEAK / near-absent for a direct link.**
- **ADHD masking/camouflaging:** the evidence is **qualitative and mostly in women**, not controlled:
  - Van der Putten WJ, et al. (2024). "Is camouflaging unique for autism? A comparison of camouflaging between adults with autism and ADHD." *Autism Research* 17. https://doi.org/10.1002/aur.3099 **[WEAK-MODERATE — cross-sectional comparison; the strongest single masking-in-ADHD paper found]**
  - McKinney A, et al. (2024). "Camouflaging in neurodivergent and neurotypical girls at the transition to adolescence…" *JCPP Advances*. https://doi.org/10.1002/jcv2.12294 **[WEAK — participatory/qualitative, mixed neurodivergent sample]**
  - Qualitative interview/thematic work (e.g., "Behind Her Eyes: Masking in Women with ADHD," UCD thesis repository; "Experiences of social camouflaging in adults with ADHD," *Research in Developmental Disabilities*) — **[WEAK — small qualitative samples, self-report]**
- **"Compensatory over-functioning / people-pleasing / caretaking as ADHD masking": CLINICAL-CONCEPT.** Widely described by clinicians and in lived-experience writing; **no controlled studies establish a Self-Sacrifice-schema-specific mechanism.** The camouflaging construct itself was imported from the autism literature and is only recently being tested in ADHD.

**Section 4 bottom line:** "There are real, interviewed people describing masking and over-functioning, and a small emerging quantitative literature — but for the *Self-Sacrifice schema specifically*, this is clinical lore with qualitative support, not established science. Of the three schemas, this is the one to caveat hardest."

## Honest limitations of this brief (read before fact-checking)

1. **No effect sizes for the schema studies.** Neither Philipsen nor Kiraz reported *d*/η² in their abstracts, and full-text tables were not accessible. All "most elevated" claims are **rank-order**, not magnitude. Do not state a numeric effect size for any schema.
2. **Unresolved Philipsen discrepancy (Section 1 flag).** Primary abstract says one non-significant schema (Vulnerability to Harm); a same-group secondary review lists five (adding Self-Sacrifice, Insufficient Self-Control, Enmeshment, Approval-Seeking). Not adjudicable without full text. This directly affects the Insufficient-Self-Control and Self-Sacrifice claims — present it as contested.
3. **Citations not fully verified, flagged inline:** the Eakin 2004 page range, the Barkley/Murphy/Fischer 2008 divorce statistics (book, not indexed trial), and the 2026 "boredom-ADHD" review — verify page/DOI before publication.
4. **The Dodson "98–99% of ADHD have RSD" figure has no published dataset found.** Do not repeat it as fact.
5. Sonuga-Barke's dual-pathway model has a 2002 (*Behavioural Brain Research*) and a 2003 (*Neurosci Biobehav Rev*) statement; the 2003 elaboration is cited here. Both are legitimate; pick per space.

**One-line verdict for the motto:** Emotional Deprivation has the best (still modest) schema-level support; Insufficient Self-Control is *disputed between the two studies* but sits atop a STRONG self-regulation science; Self-Sacrifice is the weakest and largely clinical lore. "Interest-based nervous system" and "RSD" are useful clinician framings pointing at real, well-evidenced phenomena — but as named constructs they are unvalidated, unmeasured, and absent from the DSM-5-TR.
